Far fields

Leaf far-field patterns and the collections that store them.

A leaf's far field is its radiated (or received) pattern sampled on that leaf's own direction grid, referenced to its own box centre. The representation a given operator needs is chosen by MLFMA.farfieldtype; the storage a collection uses is a separate choice, and the compressed harmonic storages have their own pages under Harmonics.

MLFMA.FarFields — Module
MLFMA.FarFields

The far-field representations, the leaf collections built on them, and the kernels that multiply by them.

Owns AbstractFarField and the concrete representation types, their component accessors, the mirror and coupling rules that are representation-local, the radiation/receive/lazy-receive collections, and the leaf multiply kernels.

Assembly owns the collection builders. It chooses the collection family, assembler, side, and storage. Operators owns the store-rule table. Keeping those responsibilities outside this layer avoids cyclic dependencies between submodules.

Kernels ask operators about their coupling through Seams, which declares the generics that Operators implements.

MLFMA.FarFields.SHEBATCHTHRESHOLD — Constant
SHEBATCHTHRESHOLD

Leaves per batch below which the spherical-harmonic leaf multiply falls back to one group at a time.

The crossing against the sampled leaf multiply was measured between 16 and 32 groups. At 16 groups, runtime was 1.17x and 1.54x of baseline across two runs; at 32 it was 0.38x and 0.41x. The threshold enables batching at 16. See benchmark/interpolation/shewrite.jl.

This controls whether to batch. SHEPROJECTIONTHREADTHRESHOLD separately controls whether projection in a batch flush may run in parallel. A measured 2.8x jump across 15 -> 16 leaves comes from enabling batching, not from the projection threshold.

MLFMA.FarFields.SHEPROJECTIONTHREADTHRESHOLD — Constant
SHEPROJECTIONTHREADTHRESHOLD

Groups in a single flush below which the projection runs serially regardless of the scheduler H2Factory.LeafMulOp resolves to.

This differs from SHEBATCHTHRESHOLD: a chunk larger than scratch capacity flushes at maxgroups and again for any remainder. That final flush can contain a single group, too little work to justify spawning projection tasks.

The two thresholds currently match because they use the same benchmark sweep, but they can be tuned independently.

MLFMA.FarFields.LazyReceiveFieldCollection — Type
LazyReceiveFieldCollection(operator, farfieldcollection, mirrorindices)

The test side of a leaf, derived from the trial side instead of assembled.

This H2Factory leaf moment collection requires a leaf scratch buffer. It is not matrix-like. It implements the two applyleafmul! arities used by the traversal, but no mul!/conjmul! of its own.

It wraps the RADIATION collection and derives the receive side on demand from mirrorindices and receivecouplingrule, so the test side is never assembled or stored.

The workspace must provide a leaf channel. It owns one far field per (level, chunk) (see leafscratchchannel), so mirroring into a real far field and calling BLAS is allocation-free, and measured 27-45x faster than a fused loop (see benchmark/README.md).

There is no fallback without a leaf channel. H2Factory.buildleafchannels defaults to EmptyChannels(), so the core must implement it. _requiresleafscratch reports a specific error when scratch is missing.

The operator and the wrapped collection must agree on representation. A collection of a different representation would be indexed with component indices meant for another layout and would silently produce wrong numbers wherever those indices happen to be in range, which they always are once the component counts coincide. That is checked once at construction rather than in the per-sample kernels.

MLFMA.FarFields.VectorSphericalHarmonicStorage — Type
VectorSphericalHarmonicStorage(order, center, coefficients)

One group's basis-function far-field patterns as tangential vector-harmonic coefficients.

coefficients is 2·numvshcoefficients(order) × nbasisfunctions, column j holding basis function j's X-family coefficients followed by its Ψ-family ones. Contiguous per basis function and contiguous per family, which is what lets the aggregation be one GEMV and the synthesis read each family as a submatrix view rather than a gather.

center is the expansion center the coefficients are referenced to; patterns about a different center differ by exp(-iκ k̂·Δc), which is not a coefficient-space operation, so only collections sharing a center may be summed. Same contract as SphericalHarmonicStorage.

H2Factory.Interfaces.applyleafmul! — Method
H2Factory.applyleafmul!(scratch, y, collection, moment, α, β)

Scatter an incoming moment onto a leaf's degrees of freedom: y .= α · Cᵀ Mᵀ Kᵀ moment + β · y.

The transpose of the receive collection's aggregation, which is what Rec = K M C means concretely. β arrives as false from the traversal's overwriting leaf scatter, which is why the accumulation short-circuits on it rather than multiplying by zero.

source
H2Factory.Interfaces.applyleafmul! — Method
H2Factory.applyleafmul!(scratch, moment, collection, x)

Aggregate a leaf: moment .= S · (C x).

First, A = C x applies one GEMV to the coefficient matrix, with (2M+1)·D rows rather than Nφ·D. Synthesis then writes into the moment's component arrays. It needs no frame projection or intermediate array of samples.

source
H2Factory.Interfaces.applyleafmul! — Method
H2Factory.applyleafmul!(scratch, y, collection, moment, α, β)

Scatter an incoming moment onto a leaf's degrees of freedom: y .= α * Cᵀ Mᵀ Pᵀ moment + β * y.

The transpose of SphericalHarmonicRadiationCollection's aggregation with M for S, which is what Rec = Π R means when the operator's coupling is the identity. The displayed formula assumes that case; the implementation also applies the transpose of a nonidentity coupling. See this file's receive-side header.

α/β follow LinearAlgebra.mul!'s convention, and β arrives as false from the traversal's overwriting leaf scatter, which is why the accumulation below short-circuits on it rather than multiplying by zero.

source
H2Factory.Interfaces.applyleafmul! — Method
H2Factory.applyleafmul!(scratch, moment, collection, x)

Aggregate a leaf: moment .= project(S · (C x)).

Aggregation has three steps:

  1. A = C x, one GEMV against the coefficient matrix, 3(P+1)² rows rather than 2NΩ. This reads the compressed data and reduces traffic for the memory-bound leaf multiply.
  2. Cart = S · reshape(A, ncoeff, 3), synthesis onto the level's directions. S is shared by every leaf at the level, so batching this step across a chunk reduces repeated matrix loads.
  3. the frame projection, 3 Cartesian components down to (θ̂, φ̂).

_applycircularcoupling!(moment, K) -> moment

Mix a moment's components in place by K. The aggregation-side counterpart of the fold in _coupledgathermoment!.

_applycoupling!(moment, K) -> moment

Mix a moment's two tangential components in place by K, the operator's receive coupling.

The aggregation-side counterpart of the fold in _coupledbackprojectinto!. In place and with no temporary: the two components are read into scalars before either is written, so a swap (MWDoubleLayer3D) is safe.

MLFMA.FarFields._cheaggregate! — Method
_cheaggregate!(moment, scratch, storage, x, Y) -> moment

moment = Y C x, the leaf aggregation for whichever side owns synthesis matrix Y.

Y is scratch.synthesis on the radiation side and scratch.mirror on the Galerkin receive side. The receive caller applies the operator's component coupling after this kernel.

Writes one component at a time straight into the moment, with no (Nφ × D) intermediate: the spherical version needs one because a frame projection has to read all three Cartesian components together, and there is nothing to project here.

MLFMA.FarFields._chescatter! — Function
_chescatter!(y, scratch, storage, moment, Y, α, β, mode, K=I) -> y

y = α (K Y C)ᵀ moment + β y, or the adjoint of the same when mode is _Adjointed().

Three stages mirroring _cheaggregate! in reverse: the coupling transpose, the reversed synthesis, and the reversed coefficient multiply. Unlike aggregation, this stages into scratch.samples, because the reversed synthesis is one GEMM over all D components and needs them contiguous, a moment's components are separate arrays.

MLFMA.FarFields._coupledgathermoment! — Method
_coupledgathermoment!(G, moment, K) -> G

Gather a moment's components into G while mixing them by K: G[:, d] = Σ_e K[e, d] * moment[e], i.e. Kᵀ applied across components.

The coupled counterpart of _gathermoment!, which gathers without mixing. Each output is accumulated in a scalar before it is written to G. Inputs are read from moment; the caller must keep that storage separate from G when the coupling mixes components.

MLFMA.FarFields._vshaggregate! — Method
_vshaggregate!(moment, scratch, storage, x, blocks) -> moment

moment = B C x, with B = [[𝒫, s𝒬], [𝒬, -s𝒫]] the block operator of whichever side owns blocks.

Two stages and no third: the coefficient GEMV, then the synthesis. There is no frame projection, that is the structural saving over SHE, whose synthesis lands in Cartesian components and needs a third pass to reach the tangential moment.

MLFMA.FarFields._vshscatter! — Function
_vshscatter!(y, scratch, storage, moment, blocks, α, β, mode) -> y

y = α (B C)ᵀ moment + β y, or the adjoint of the same when mode is _Adjointed().

Bᵀ = [[𝒫ᵀ, 𝒬ᵀ], [s𝒬ᵀ, -s𝒫ᵀ]], and Bᴴ is the same with s unchanged because the sign is real. No quadrature weights are applied at the leaf. This is a moment collection, not a testing functional; see the receive-side header in sphericalharmoniccollection.jl.

MLFMA.FarFields.circularreceivecoupling — Method
circularreceivecoupling(op, F) -> SMatrix{D,D}

The D × D component map a compressed 2-D receive collection applies after its mirrored synthesis: receivecouplingrule(op, F) composed with mirrorrule(F), both as matrices.

MLFMA.FarFields.projectionscheduler — Method
projectionscheduler(scheduler, filled)

scheduler, or SerialScheduler() when the flush is too small to be worth spawning for.

The scheduler selects the map's policy for nested parallelism. The threshold determines whether this flush is large enough to benefit. A tail of one group runs serially regardless of that policy. See SHEPROJECTIONTHREADTHRESHOLD.
