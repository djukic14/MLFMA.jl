Circular harmonics

The 2-D counterpart. The circle has no Jacobian, so its storage and mirror rules differ from the spherical case.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics — Module
MLFMA.CircularHarmonics

Orthonormal circular harmonics ψ_m(φ) = exp(imφ)/√(2π) and the transform pair over a set of sampling angles (the 2-D counterpart of MLFMA.Harmonics.SphericalHarmonics).

MLFMA samples each leaf basis function's far-field pattern at the finest level's angular rate. The translation operator sets that rate for the whole box. An individual basis function has smaller support and needs less bandwidth. Storing harmonic coefficients removes that redundancy.

The measured benefits differ between 2-D and 3-D:

  • 3-D compresses 10.4x at ε = 1e-4 and is FASTER (0.57x serial, 0.22x threaded), because the sampled leaf multiply there is memory-bound – 4.5 MB per group at ~0.5 flop/byte.
  • 2-D compresses 1.47x at ε = 1e-5 and is 1.67x SLOWER on ordinary fixtures, because a 2-D leaf block is ~3.5 KB and never leaves cache. It reaches break-even only above ~64 basis functions per leaf and never gets past 0.89x; BLAS threading makes the low-occupancy case worse rather than better.

Both halves of that are the same O(L) versus O(L²) asymmetry, which shows up in three places at once: 2L+1 coefficients against (L+1)², 2(L+1) directions against 2(L+1)·4(L+1), and O(boxsize/h) basis functions in a box on a curve against O((boxsize/h)²) in a box on a surface. The first of those is why 2-D can never win as widely however the constants fall: the sphere's product grid spends 8(L+1)² samples on an (L+1)²-dimensional space, so SHE is 5.33x ahead before any bandwidth margin, while 2(L+1) samples against 2M+1 coefficients is a dead heat at M = L.

This backend provides the same storage option as the 3-D backend. Select it to save memory at roughly break-even time, not for speed. MLFMA.LEAFSTORAGERECOM remains SampledLeafStorage. See MLFMA.Assembly.CircularHarmonicLeafStorage for the crossover table.

The MLFMA grids do not change. Aggregation between levels, translation, and interpolation/anterpolation still use the ordinary CircularSampling grids. Harmonics appear only between the MoM basis and the leaf group. The leaf grid must resolve the translation bandwidth, not just the pattern bandwidth. Storing fewer coefficients and expanding on demand preserves that grid.

This submodule can be tested without a tree or assembler. Orthonormality, Parseval, and closed-form modes provide analytic checks. The submodule is not exported.

using MLFMA.CircularHarmonics

t = CircularHarmonicTransform(ComplexF64, sampling, 7)
a = analyze(t, F)             # F: samples (rows) x components (columns)
F′ = synthesize(t, a)         # back to samples
truncationorder(a, 1e-5)      # the order that tail actually needs

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.CHETAILFLOOR — Constant
CHETAILFLOOR

The smallest relative tail leveltruncationorder will ask for, 1e-7.

This matches SphericalHarmonics.SHETAILFLOOR because the numerical limit does not depend on geometry. The tail criterion compares against τ², so τ = 1e-8 demands a relative energy below 1e-16, near round-off in Float64. Cancellation can keep the computed tail above that threshold, causing truncationorder to return the full order.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.CHETAILRATIO — Constant
CHETAILRATIO

The factor by which leveltruncationorder tightens the map's ε before reading the tail: τ = max(ε/CHETAILRATIO, CHETAILFLOOR), with CHETAILRATIO = 10.

The 2-D calibration differs from the spherical rule, which uses ε/100. benchmark/interpolation/checalibration.jl measures 20 rows: five meshcircle configurations spanning two box sizes (λ/4 and λ/2) and leaf occupancies 10, 20, 40, 40 and 81, each at ε = 1e-3, 1e-4, 1e-5 and 1e-6. M* is the smallest kept order whose worst GROUP MOMENT error meets ε:

box   N_j    ε=1e-3          ε=1e-4          ε=1e-5          ε=1e-6
            M*  r1 r10 r100  M*  r1 r10 r100  M*  r1 r10 r100  M*  r1 r10 r100
λ/4    10    6   6   7   8    7   7   8   9    8   8   9  10    9   9  10  10
λ/4    20    6   6   7   8    8   7*  8   9    9   8*  9  10   10   9* 10  10
λ/4    40    6   6   7   8    8   7*  8   9    9   8*  9  10   10   9* 10  10
λ/2    40    9   8* 10  11   10  10  11  13   12  11* 13  14   13  13  14  14
λ/2    81    9   8* 10  11   10  10  11  13   12  11* 13  14   13  13  14  14
                                                      (* = UNDER-predicts M*)

τ = ε under-predicts in 8 of the 20 rows, always by exactly 1. τ = ε/10 never under-predicts and over-predicts by at most 1. τ = ε/100 never under-predicts either but over-predicts M* by up to 3, which costs up to 17 % more coefficients than ε/10 does: at ε = 1e-4 on the λ/2 box it asks for 27 where 23 suffice, i.e. compression 1.48x instead of 1.74x.

In these measurements, the order depends mainly on box size and ε, not leaf occupancy. The λ/4 rows at 20 and 40 basis functions per leaf are identical, as are the λ/2 rows at 40 and 81; only the sparsest leaf (10) differs, and by one order. M* saturates – once a leaf holds enough functions to cover its box, the group moment's bandwidth is the BOX's, not any individual function's. That is also why this rule has support the 3-D one explicitly lacks: SHETAILFLOOR's calibration note records that all three of its valid configurations shared a single box size, so its dependence on box size is unmeasured. This one is measured across two.

The calibration measures group-moment error at the leaf, not the error of a full matvec. End-to-end measurements are needed to check whether this constant remains suitable. Under-prediction is the dangerous direction – it silently spends the map's error budget – so the rule is biased against it here exactly as it is in 3-D.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.CircularHarmonicStorage — Type
CircularHarmonicStorage(order, center, coefficients)

One group's basis-function far-field patterns, stored as circular-harmonic coefficients instead of samples.

coefficients is ((2·order+1) * ncomponents) × nbasisfunctions, with the row index running over m fastest – centered, -order:order – and the component slowest, so column j reshapes to the (ncoeff, ncomponents) matrix of basis function j's expansion at no cost.

center is the expansion center the coefficients are referenced to, an SVector{2}. Patterns about a different center differ by exp(-γ k̂·Δc), which is not a coefficient-space operation, so a group's coefficients can only be summed if they share this center – see aggregate!.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.CircularHarmonicStorage — Method
CircularHarmonicStorage(t, patterns, center) -> storage

Analyse sampled patterns into coefficients. patterns is a vector, one entry per basis function, each ndirections × ncomponents on t's grid.

t may be built on the leaf's own MLFMA grid. Oversampling was unnecessary in the measured cases. The circle's quadrature is exact for resolved modes (see transform.jl), but a real basis pattern is not band-limited, so its content above N/2 folds back onto the modes being kept instead of being dropped. benchmark/interpolation/checalibration.jl sweeps oversampling over 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 and the group-moment error is IDENTICAL TO SIX DIGITS at every order tested. The reason is that the folded content is already below the error being measured: on the λ/4 fixture the pattern's own spectrum has fallen to ~1e-14 by |m| = 16, beyond the leaf grid's Nyquist bin at 14.

Oversampling did not improve those measurements and costs a proportionally larger assembly. It is available on circulargrid because "band-limited enough by N/2" is a property of these fixtures rather than a theorem, and a caller with an unusually large box relative to its mesh should be able to check.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.CircularHarmonicTransform — Type
CircularHarmonicTransform(C, φs, weights, M)
CircularHarmonicTransform(C, sampling::MLFMA.CircularSampling, M)
CircularHarmonicTransform(C, M; oversampling=1)

The synthesis matrix S[q, m] = ψ_m(φ_q) together with the quadrature weights w_q that define its adjoint, for maximum order M and element type C <: Complex.

The harmonics are ORTHONORMAL on the circle,

ψ_m(φ) = exp(i m φ) / √(2π)        ∫ψ_m conj(ψ_m′) dφ = δ_{m m′}

so Parseval holds as Σ_m |a_m|² = ∫|f|² dφ and modeenergy is an honest L² measure. That costs a 1/√(2π) that a bare DFT does not carry, and buys the truncation rule its meaning.

φs is any vector of angles in the same linear order the sampled fields use; the sampling form reads both angles and weights from the grid, whose order is the one 2-D far fields are stored in. The third form builds the transform on circulargrid for analysis during setup.

For band-limited patterns, the circle's trapezoidal rule is exact when the grid resolves the analysis integrand. General patterns can still alias. See this file's header.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.aggregate! — Method
aggregate!(A, storage, x) -> A

A .= storage.coefficients * x – the group's aggregated expansion, A_g = Σ_j I_j a_j.

A is a vector of length numcoefficients(storage) * ncomponents(storage); reshape(A, ncoeff, D) is then the matrix synthesize wants.

Every column must share the storage's center for this sum to be meaningful. The caller is responsible for that agreement.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.aggregatelevel! — Method
aggregatelevel!(F, A, t, storages, xs) -> F

Aggregate every group of a level and synthesise them all in ONE GEMM.

storages and xs are matched vectors over the level's groups; A is scratch of size (ncoeff, D * ngroups) and F receives (ndirections, D * ngroups), group g occupying columns ((g-1)D+1):(gD) in both.

The single trailing synthesize! is the whole reason this function exists rather than a loop calling synthesize per group – see this file's header on why batching is mandatory rather than an optimization.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.analyze — Method
analyze(t, F) -> a

Project sampled values onto the harmonics: a = Sᴴ (w .* F).

This is the exact adjoint of synthesize with respect to the weighted inner product. It also recovers coefficients exactly when the angles and weights resolve the analysis integrand. Custom grids need not satisfy that condition.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.analyzelevel! — Method
analyzelevel!(B, t, G) -> B

Project a whole level's incoming fields onto the harmonics: B = Sᴴ (w .* G).

G is (ndirections, D * ngroups) as aggregatelevel! produces, and B receives (ncoeff, D * ngroups) in the same column layout. One GEMM for the level, for the same reason the forward direction batches: S is shared by every group.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.circulargrid — Method
circulargrid(M; oversampling=1) -> (φs, weights)

The angles and weights on which analysis up to order M is EXACT: NφCircular(M) = 2M+2 equidistant points with the uniform trapezoidal weight 2π/N.

Exact rather than merely convergent, because the circle carries no Jacobian: the trapezoidal rule integrates every mode |m| < N with no error, and the analysis integrand of a pattern band-limited to M reaches only 2M < N. This is MLFMA.NφCircular – the SAME rate rule the MLFMA level grids use, which is not a coincidence but the same exactness argument applied to a different product.

oversampling multiplies N to reduce aliasing. A real basis function's pattern is not band-limited at all, so its content above N/2 folds back onto the retained modes; more points push that floor down. SphericalHarmonics.gausslegendregrid is also exact for its stated band limit; oversampling helps when a pattern has content beyond that limit.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.leveltruncationorder — Method
leveltruncationorder(coefficients, ε; tailfloor=CHETAILFLOOR, ratio=CHETAILRATIO) -> Int

The expansion order to keep for a whole LEVEL: the largest order any of its boxes needs.

coefficients is an iterable of per-box coefficient matrices, each (2M+1) × ncolumns.

All boxes in a level must use one order because the leaf multiply synthesises a whole chunk against one matrix S. The maximum supplies enough coefficients for every box in the batch.

See CHETAILRATIO for what τ = max(ε/ratio, tailfloor) was calibrated against and where it differs from the spherical rule.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.mirrorsynthesis — Method
mirrorsynthesis(t) -> Matrix

S · diag((-1)^m) – the synthesis matrix with the antipodal parity folded in, so synthesising with it evaluates the expansion at -k̂ instead of at k̂.

What the Galerkin receive side uses in place of a mirror-index permutation: the mirror is DIAGONAL in coefficient space (see modeparity), so it costs one matrix built once per level rather than a gather per leaf per matvec.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.modeenergy — Method
modeenergy(a) -> Vector

Total |coefficient|² per |m|, summed over the two signs and over the columns of a, returned as a 0:M-indexed-by-position vector (entry |m|+1 is mode |m|).

Both signs together, because ±m are one physical angular rate and a truncation keeps or drops them as a pair – an expansion truncated to M′ is -M′:M′. Meaningful as an error measure because the harmonics are orthonormal: by Parseval this is the L²([0,2π)) energy of the corresponding piece of the field.

source
MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.modeparity — Method
modeparity(M) -> Vector{Int}

(-1)^m for m = -M:M, in the centered row order – the diagonal of the ANTIPODAL map in coefficient space.

ψ_m(φ + π) = e^{im(φ+π)}/√(2π) = (-1)^m ψ_m(φ), so the mirror k̂ → -k̂ that the sampled representation realises as a permutation of Nφ samples is, here, one sign per mode. The 2-D counterpart of SphericalHarmonics.parity's (-1)^l – and that is NOT a cosmetic substitution: on the sphere the sign is per DEGREE and every order within a degree shares it, while on the circle degree and order are the same index.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.numcoefficients — Method
numcoefficients(M) -> Int

2M + 1, the number of circular harmonics of order |m| ≤ M.

The 2-D counterpart of (P+1)², and the whole O(L) versus O(L²) story in one line: a degree-L pattern on a circle needs 2L+1 numbers where one on a sphere needs (L+1)².

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.truncatecoefficients — Method
truncatecoefficients(a, M) -> AbstractVecOrMat

The order-≤ M part of an expansion: the CENTERED rows (M₀-M+1):(M₀+M+1), where M₀ is the order a was analysed at.

A contiguous view and not a gather, but a center slice rather than the spherical side's leading one, because m runs -M:M here – see this file's header for why that ordering was kept. One analysis at a generous order therefore still serves every candidate order below it.

MLFMA.Harmonics.CircularHarmonics.truncationorder — Method
truncationorder(a, ε) -> Int

Smallest M whose discarded tail holds less than ε² of the total energy: Σ_{|m|>M} energy / Σ_m energy < ε².

The squaring is deliberate – ε is a relative error on the FIELD, and energy is quadratic in it, so this returns the order at which the reconstruction is accurate to about ε. Returns the full order present in a if no smaller one qualifies, and 0 for an all-zero expansion.
